# 6.15 --- Production HA Readiness and Acceptance

## Purpose

Parts 6.1--6.14 establish the individual capabilities required to
operate PostgreSQL streaming replication and high availability.

This final integrated project answers:

> **Is the complete PostgreSQL HA environment technically and
> operationally ready for production?**

A connected standby is not sufficient evidence of HA readiness.
Production acceptance requires evidence across database authority,
replication, WAL receive/replay, WAL retention, replication slots,
durability policy, standby conflicts, topology, capacity, storage,
archiving, monitoring, alerting, backup/restore, planned switchover,
unplanned failover, fencing, RPO, rejoin/rebuild, failback policy,
operational access, and ownership.

The objective is not merely to prove that replication works. The
objective is to prove that the environment can be operated safely when
something fails.

## Safety Boundary

The SQL acceptance lab is observational only:

``` text
OBSERVE
   ↓
COLLECT
   ↓
CORRELATE
   ↓
PRODUCE EVIDENCE
```

It must not perform promotion, replay pause/resume, slot
creation/deletion, configuration changes, session termination,
checkpoint, vacuum, reindex, DDL/DML, `pg_rewind`, service control,
routing, fencing, switchover, or failover.

Disruptive capabilities are validated using controlled evidence from
Parts 6.12--6.14.

## Credential Safety

Acceptance evidence must not expose passwords, tokens, secret
environment variables, `primary_conninfo`, or credential-bearing
`pg_stat_wal_receiver.conninfo`.

## Service Health and HA Health

Always distinguish service availability from HA protection.

For example, a healthy application and primary with an unavailable
required standby means:

``` text
SERVICE = HEALTHY
HA      = DEGRADED
```

not that the complete environment is healthy.

## Acceptance Outcomes

Use only:

-   **PASS**
-   **PASS WITH DOCUMENTED DEGRADATION**
-   **BLOCKED**
-   **NOT APPLICABLE**

A degradation requires explicit risk ownership, mitigation, monitoring,
remediation, target date, and approval. `NOT APPLICABLE` requires
architecture-based justification.

## Evidence Rules

Every acceptance decision should identify what was checked, where, when,
what was expected, what was observed, supporting evidence, and
remediation ownership.

Avoid statements such as "replication looks fine." Prefer explicit role,
observation time, receive/replay positions, slot, sync state, expected
state, observed state, result, and evidence.

## SQL Execution Context

The companion SQL classifies checks as:

``` text
[UNIVERSAL]
[PRIMARY-ORIENTED]
[STANDBY-ORIENTED]
[STANDBY-ONLY]
[CONDITIONAL]
```

Every evidence package begins with server identity, observation time,
version, and `pg_is_in_recovery()`.

## Role Validation

`pg_is_in_recovery()` is the database role authority for this acceptance
workflow:

``` text
false → Primary
true  → Standby / Recovery
```

Do not infer role solely from hostname, DNS, Kubernetes pod name, VM
name, or historical architecture.

An unexpected dual-primary observation is a hard blocker until database
authority and split-brain risk are resolved.

## Streaming Replication

Primary evidence comes from `pg_stat_replication`.

Standby evidence comes from `pg_stat_wal_receiver`,
`pg_last_wal_receive_lsn()`, and `pg_last_wal_replay_lsn()`.

Key rule:

``` text
RECEIVED != REPLAYED
```

Where practical, capture both sides of the replication relationship.

A single LSN snapshot can prove state but may not prove progression.
Active systems may require repeated observations. Idle systems can
legitimately show unchanged LSNs.

Do not invent universal lag thresholds.

## Replay Pause State

`pg_get_wal_replay_pause_state()` is a **STANDBY-ONLY** check.

Normal production recovery should normally report:

``` text
not paused
```

Unexpected `pause requested` or `paused` requires investigation and
blocks normal standby acceptance until understood or resolved.

## Replication Slots and WAL Retention

Inspect slot ownership, activity, `restart_lsn`, `wal_status`,
`safe_wal_size`, `inactive_since`, `invalidation_reason`, `failover`,
and `synced`.

An inactive slot is not automatically bad. Determine its owner,
consumer, purpose, retention impact, and required remediation.

A required slot with `invalidation_reason IS NOT NULL` represents a
serious protection failure.

Interpret `safe_wal_size` contextually. `NULL` is not automatically
unhealthy.

`wal_keep_size` contributes a minimum retention amount; it is not a
maximum-retention guarantee.

WAL-retention acceptance requires both database and platform storage
evidence.

## Synchronous Replication and RPO

Correlate:

``` text
synchronous_standby_names
synchronous_commit
application_name
sync_state
```

A connected standby does not prove synchronous durability.

`synchronous_standby_names` alone does not prove that every transaction
receives synchronous protection. Acceptance must reflect the actual
commit policy and application/session behavior.

RPO must be explicitly declared. Do not make unsupported zero-data-loss
claims.

## Standby Conflicts

`pg_stat_database_conflicts` provides accumulated recovery-conflict
evidence.

A nonzero historical counter does not automatically mean the standby is
currently unhealthy.

Evaluate frequency, trend, workload impact, monitoring, alerting, query
policy, `hot_standby_feedback`, and standby-delay policy.

## Cascading Replication

Where cascading replication is used, validate the expected
upstream/downstream relationships, receive/replay progression,
intermediate-node capacity, failure dependency, and monitoring.

Where it is not used, record **NOT APPLICABLE** with architecture
justification.

## Logical Failover Protection

Physical HA health does not prove logical failover readiness.

Where logical failover slots are used, validate their required state and
securely validate applicable synchronization prerequisites without
printing `primary_conninfo`.

## Capacity

Review connection capacity, WAL sender capacity, replication-slot
capacity, topology growth, and platform headroom.

The SQL exposes facts but does not invent universal utilization
thresholds.

## Archive Readiness

Where WAL archiving is required, validate current progression, recent
success, failure history, monitoring, and restore usage.

Historical archive failures alone do not prove a current outage. A
currently stalled required archive path blocks the recovery capability
depending on it.

## Monitoring and Alerting

Monitoring should cover database role, replication connectivity,
receive/replay, slots, WAL retention, archive health, storage,
connections, standby conflicts, synchronous-state changes, node/service
availability, and backup failures.

Monitoring must follow roles rather than assuming one host remains
primary forever.

Critical alerts require an owner, runbook, and escalation path.

## Backup and Restore

Validate backup schedule, target, retention, role awareness,
credentials, monitoring, restore procedure, recent successful backup,
and restore-test evidence.

Replication does not replace backups. A successful backup job alone does
not prove recoverability.

## Planned Switchover Readiness

Reuse controlled evidence from **6.12 --- Planned Switchover**.

Validate evidence for pre-flight, writer quiescence, final WAL boundary,
candidate replay, fencing, GO/NO-GO, promotion verification, database
smoke test, routing, and restored HA.

6.15 does not perform another disruptive switchover merely to collect
acceptance evidence.

## Unplanned Failover and Split-Brain Prevention

Reuse evidence from **6.13 --- Unplanned Failover and Split-Brain
Prevention**.

Hard rule:

``` text
PRIMARY UNREACHABLE != PRIMARY FENCED
```

Fencing states:

``` text
FENCED
NOT FENCED
UNKNOWN
```

`UNKNOWN` is not safe promotion authorization.

Document the fencing mechanism, authority, execution path, verification
method, required access, and runbook.

Failure detection is not promotion authorization.

## Promotion Authority

Document who declares incident authority, authorizes emergency
promotion, executes promotion, confirms fencing, and changes application
routing.

## RPO and Ambiguous Transactions

Record the replication mode, RPO objective, expected transaction-loss
exposure, synchronous/commit policy, application reconciliation
strategy, and failure assumptions.

During failover, a client error does not necessarily prove a transaction
did not commit. Where relevant, production readiness includes
idempotency, duplicate protection, retry policy, and transaction
reconciliation.

## Rejoin, Rewind, and Rebuild

Reuse evidence from **6.14 --- Rejoin, Rewind, and Failback**.

Evidence should demonstrate former-primary quarantine,
authoritative-primary declaration, evidence preservation, rewind
eligibility, source/target verification, dry-run capability,
rewind/rebuild decision, configuration validation, standby recovery
validation, and HA restoration.

Operators must understand:

``` text
pg_rewind is destructive to TARGET

SOURCE = authoritative primary
TARGET = quarantined former primary
```

A rebuild procedure is a first-class recovery path and must exist even
when rewind is normally preferred.

## Failback Policy

Hard rule:

``` text
REJOIN != FAILBACK
```

A healthy `B Primary → A Standby` topology may remain final.

If failback is required, it is a new planned change using 6.12 controls
after stabilization and separate authorization.

## Operational Access

Safely validate required access for database, host/platform, monitoring,
backup, routing, fencing, and secret management.

Emergency credentials must exist, be retrievable by authorized
responders, and have documented audit requirements without placing the
credentials in acceptance evidence.

## Runbook Availability

Critical replication, WAL/slot, conflict, monitoring, switchover,
failover, fencing, rejoin, rewind/rebuild, backup recovery, and routing
procedures must remain accessible during database failure.

## Non-Degradable Blockers

Examples include unknown database authority, unresolved split brain,
unknown required fencing capability, promotion without required
split-brain controls, no former-primary recovery path, no rebuild
capability, no authoritative backup/recovery capability, and missing
critical operational access.

## Key Takeaways

1.  A connected standby does not prove production HA readiness.
2.  HA acceptance requires database and operational evidence.
3.  The acceptance SQL is SAFE-READ.
4.  Every evidence package identifies its execution node.
5.  Database roles are discovered rather than inferred.
6.  Expected architecture is declared before observed state is judged.
7.  Receive and replay are different stages.
8.  Universal lag thresholds should not be invented.
9.  Slot and WAL-retention evidence requires contextual interpretation.
10. WAL-retention acceptance requires platform storage evidence.
11. Synchronous durability requires intended-versus-observed validation.
12. RPO must be explicitly defined.
13. Conflict counters are cumulative evidence, not instantaneous failure
    indicators.
14. Logical failover readiness is separate from physical HA.
15. Monitoring follows database roles.
16. Alerts require ownership, runbooks, and escalation.
17. Replication does not replace backups.
18. Backup readiness includes restore evidence.
19. 6.12 provides planned-switchover evidence.
20. 6.13 provides failover, fencing, and split-brain-prevention
    evidence.
21. Primary unreachable does not mean primary fenced.
22. Failure detection is not promotion authorization.
23. 6.14 provides rejoin, rewind, rebuild, and failback controls.
24. Rebuild is a first-class recovery path.
25. Rejoin does not require failback.
26. Operational access and ownership must be proven before an incident.
27. Degraded acceptance requires explicit risk ownership.
28. Some safety controls remain hard blockers.
29. Sign-off cannot override unresolved technical blockers.
30. Part 6 completes only after final repository and tracker validation.

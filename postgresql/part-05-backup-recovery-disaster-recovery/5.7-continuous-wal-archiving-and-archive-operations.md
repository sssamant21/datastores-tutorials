# Part 5.7 — Continuous WAL Archiving and Archive Operations

**Target:** PostgreSQL 18  
**Status:** Revised Final / Canonical Edition  
**Track:** Part 5 — Backup, Recovery & Disaster Recovery

## 1. Purpose

Continuous WAL archiving preserves completed Write-Ahead Log (WAL) segments outside PostgreSQL's active WAL area so that retained physical backups can be replayed forward during recovery.

```text
PostgreSQL workload
       ↓
   WAL generation
       ↓
     pg_wal
       ↓
 PostgreSQL archiver
       ↓
archive_command OR archive_library
       ↓
durable WAL archive
       ↓
recovery / PITR / DR
```

The fundamental operating rule is:

```text
archive configured != archive healthy != recovery validated
```

## 2. Recovery Relationship

A physical base backup establishes a recovery foundation. Continuous WAL history can extend recovery beyond that backup.

```text
base backup
    +
required continuous WAL
    ↓
recoverable physical history
```

A collection of WAL files is useful only when it contains the WAL required by the chosen recovery path.

## 3. Native PostgreSQL Archiving Prerequisites

Native continuous archiving requires an appropriate WAL level, archive mode, and an archive mechanism.

Conceptually:

```text
wal_level = replica or higher
archive_mode enabled
        +
archive_command
        OR
archive_library
```

`archive_command` and `archive_library` represent alternative archive mechanisms rather than two mechanisms that normally need to operate together.

## 4. `archive_command`

`archive_command` invokes an external command/program to archive a completed WAL segment.

Common substitutions include:

```text
%p → path of WAL file to archive
%f → WAL file name
%% → literal percent character
```

A production command must be designed for correctness, retry behavior, security, conflict detection, observability, and destination durability.

Do not treat a simplistic copy command as a universal production design.

## 5. `archive_library`

`archive_library` allows PostgreSQL to use an archive module instead of a shell command.

```text
archive_command → external command/program integration
archive_library → PostgreSQL archive module
```

The correct mechanism depends on platform and operational requirements.

## 6. Archive Success Contract

The archive mechanism's success result is a recovery-critical contract.

```text
archive mechanism reports success
            ↓
PostgreSQL can regard segment as archived
            ↓
segment may later become recyclable/removable
```

Therefore:

```text
archive success
must mean
the WAL artifact satisfies the approved archive contract
```

Never intentionally return false success merely to stop local WAL accumulation.

## 7. Retry and Idempotency

Archiving can be retried. Archive implementations should therefore be safe when the destination object already exists.

```text
destination absent
      ↓
archive safely

destination already exists
      ↓
validate expected identity/integrity
      ↓
handle safely

NOT → blind overwrite
```

Unexpected content conflicts must not be silently ignored.

## 8. Archive Status

PostgreSQL tracks archive status beneath its WAL area, conceptually associated with WAL waiting for archival and WAL whose archival has completed.

Operators may encounter:

```text
pg_wal/archive_status/
```

Filesystem details are implementation/operations evidence and are deliberately excluded from the SAFE-READ acceptance artifact.

## 9. `pg_stat_archiver`

Use `pg_stat_archiver` for PostgreSQL-visible archive activity evidence, including:
- archived count;
- last archived WAL;
- last archive time;
- failed count;
- last failed WAL;
- last failure time;
- statistics reset time.

But:

```text
archived_count increasing != destination durability proven
```

External archive validation is still required.

## 10. Archive Failure Model

Archive failure can create both availability and recovery risk.

```text
                    archive failure
                          │
              ┌───────────┴───────────┐
              ↓                       ↓
       availability risk         recovery risk
              ↓                       ↓
       WAL retained locally      continuity risk
              ↓                       ↓
         pg_wal growth            PITR impact
              ↓
       filesystem exhaustion
```

## 11. False-Success Failure Mode

A particularly dangerous implementation is:

```text
WAL not safely preserved
        +
archive mechanism reports success
        ↓
PostgreSQL believes archival succeeded
        ↓
local WAL may eventually be reusable
        ↓
required recovery artifact may be lost
```

This can appear operationally healthy while silently weakening recovery.

## 12. Archive Failure Triage

```text
archive alert
    ↓
1. Establish database/storage health
2. Determine remaining pg_wal headroom
3. Measure WAL generation rate
4. Inspect pg_stat_archiver
5. Check archive destination availability
6. Check authentication/authorization
7. Check network/DNS/service dependencies
8. Check destination capacity/quota
9. Check replication slots and replicas
10. Restore archive functionality
11. Confirm backlog drains
12. Validate WAL continuity
13. Revalidate recovery posture
14. Preserve incident evidence
```

## 13. Emergency Prohibitions

Do not respond to archive-related WAL pressure with:

```text
manual deletion from pg_wal
pg_resetwal as cleanup
blind replication-slot deletion
false-success archive commands
blind archive deletion
durability weakening
uncontrolled archive configuration changes
```

Protect recoverability while resolving the actual cause.

## 14. `archive_timeout`

On low-WAL systems with an archive-freshness requirement, `archive_timeout` may help bound how long a partially filled WAL segment remains before an archive opportunity is created.

Conceptually:

```text
low WAL generation
       +
archive freshness objective
       ↓
archive_timeout consideration
```

More aggressive switching can increase archive churn and storage/network overhead. It is not a universal performance tuning parameter.

## 15. Local WAL Capacity

Archive-outage tolerance must be capacity planned.

```text
required local headroom
≈ peak WAL generation rate
× tolerated archive outage
+ other retention requirements
+ operational safety margin
```

Also consider replication slots, standbys, backup duration, checkpoint behavior, workload bursts, and incident-response time.

## 16. Archive Destination Capacity

Archive storage planning should consider:

```text
measured WAL generation
× retention duration
+ workload growth
+ compression/representation overhead
+ versioning/immutability overhead
+ safety margin
```

Use measured workload evidence rather than a universal WAL-to-database-size ratio.

## 17. WAL Continuity

Recovery depends on required continuity.

```text
Base Backup B1
      │
      ├── WAL 001 ✓
      ├── WAL 002 ✓
      ├── WAL 003 ✗ MISSING
      ├── WAL 004 ✓
      └── WAL 005 ✓
```

If the recovery path requires WAL 003:

```text
4 of 5 files available != usable continuous recovery chain
```

Archive object count is not a sufficient recovery metric.

## 18. Retention and Cleanup

Archived WAL retention must be dependency aware.

```text
retained base backups
       +
recovery windows
       +
required WAL
       +
timelines
       +
compliance policy
       ↓
safe retention boundary
```

```text
old WAL != automatically disposable WAL
```

`pg_archivecleanup` is destructive archive-maintenance tooling and does not belong in a SAFE-READ acceptance lab.

## 19. Archive Destination Controls

A production destination should address:
- durability;
- encryption;
- access control;
- capacity;
- retention;
- deletion protection;
- failure-domain separation;
- auditability;
- retrievability;
- monitoring.

Immutability/versioning can add protection where appropriate, but lifecycle and recovery requirements must still be intentionally designed.

## 20. Security

Treat WAL archives as sensitive recovery data.

Protect:
- credentials and tokens;
- encryption keys;
- transport;
- destination permissions;
- deletion controls;
- audit evidence;
- recovery access.

Do not expose raw archive commands/libraries, embedded credentials, or sensitive implementation details in Git, tickets, screenshots, routine acceptance output, or chat.

## 21. Monitoring

Combine PostgreSQL evidence with infrastructure/provider telemetry.

```text
PostgreSQL
├── pg_stat_archiver
├── pg_stat_wal
├── pg_replication_slots
└── pg_stat_replication

Infrastructure/provider
├── pg_wal storage utilization
├── archive destination availability
├── destination capacity/quota
├── network/service health
└── backup/PITR service health
```

Time since last successful archive is useful only when interpreted with WAL generation and workload activity.

## 22. Production Change Gate

Before changing archive configuration:

```text
[ ] approved scope/change
[ ] destination validated
[ ] authentication validated
[ ] authorization validated
[ ] encryption validated
[ ] capacity validated
[ ] monitoring ready
[ ] rollback defined
[ ] current recovery posture understood
[ ] known-good configuration retained
[ ] non-production validation completed
```

After the change:

```text
[ ] archive activity observed
[ ] failures checked
[ ] destination objects verified externally
[ ] backlog behavior checked
[ ] pg_wal headroom checked
[ ] replication checked
[ ] continuity checked
[ ] recovery rehearsal scheduled/completed
```

## 23. Rollback

Rollback is more than restoring an old parameter.

```text
restore known-good mechanism
        ↓
confirm archival resumes
        ↓
confirm backlog drains safely
        ↓
verify required WAL continuity
        ↓
confirm recovery capability
```

## 24. Managed-Service Boundary

```text
self-managed PostgreSQL
    → native archive configuration
    → PostgreSQL + OS/storage telemetry

managed PostgreSQL
    → provider-managed backup/PITR may replace native workflow
    → restricted filesystem/configuration access
    → provider-specific controls and evidence
```

Do not apply self-managed filesystem procedures blindly to managed PostgreSQL.

## 25. Kubernetes / Container Platforms

For Kubernetes-hosted PostgreSQL:
- understand WAL PVC capacity and expansion;
- alert on storage pressure;
- avoid ephemeral storage for recovery artifacts;
- understand operator-managed archive behavior;
- protect archive credentials using the platform's secret mechanism;
- validate archive destination independently of pod lifecycle;
- rehearse recovery independently of pod recreation.

## 26. Recovery Evidence Hierarchy

```text
Level 1 — Archive configuration exists
Level 2 — PostgreSQL reports archive successes
Level 3 — Expected archive objects exist
Level 4 — Integrity and continuity validated
Level 5 — Required WAL retrieval validated
Level 6 — Isolated recovery succeeds
Level 7 — PostgreSQL validation succeeds
Level 8 — Application validation succeeds
Level 9 — Measured RPO/RTO satisfies approved targets
```

```text
archive job green != disaster recovery ready
```

## 28. Acceptance Boundary

The lab proves only that selected PostgreSQL-visible archive configuration and statistics can be inspected safely.

It does not prove destination durability, archive integrity, WAL continuity, correct retention, successful retrieval, PITR, or application recovery.

```text
SAFE-READ != WAL-ARCHIVE-VALIDATED
```

## 29. Key Takeaways

```text
archive configured != archive healthy
archive healthy != recovery validated
false archive success can silently destroy recoverability
archive failure can become pg_wal disk-full risk
WAL archive is sensitive recovery data
retention must follow recovery dependencies
WAL object count != continuous usable history
cleanup must not precede recovery analysis
restore testing is stronger evidence than job success
SAFE-READ != WAL-ARCHIVE-VALIDATED
```

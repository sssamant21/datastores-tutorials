# Part 5.15 — Integrated Project: Design, Validate, and Rehearse a Recovery Strategy

**Part:** 5 — Backup, Recovery & Disaster Recovery  
**Target:** PostgreSQL 18  
**Status:** Revised Final / Canonical Edition  
**Acceptance artifact:** `labs/disaster-recovery-acceptance.sql`

## 1. Project Goal

Design and rehearse an evidence-driven PostgreSQL recovery strategy that integrates the controls developed throughout Part 5: logical and physical backup, WAL archiving, PITR, replication/WAL retention, backup validation, RPO/RTO and capacity planning, HA/DR boundaries, and the production recovery runbook.

The project is complete only when the team can explain **what it can recover, from which failure, to which point, using which artifacts, under whose authority, with what measured result**.

> The companion SQL artifact is `[TUTORIAL-ACCEPTANCE — SAFE-READ]`. It collects PostgreSQL-visible evidence only. The actual restore/DR rehearsal must use an approved isolated/disposable target and architecture-specific procedures.

## 2. Scenario

Assume a production PostgreSQL service supports a critical application.

Design for at least these events:

| Scenario | Expected protection path |
|---|---|
| Primary instance failure | HA/failover where designed |
| Availability-zone failure | HA or DR according to architecture |
| Accidental table deletion | Logical recovery or PITR |
| Bad application batch | PITR/candidate restore |
| Storage corruption | Failover or validated backup recovery |
| Stalled replication consumer | Slot/WAL-retention response |
| Region/site loss | DR |
| Administrative/security compromise | Independent trusted recovery path |
| Delayed discovery of bad data | Retention + historical recovery |

Do not assume one mechanism solves every scenario.

## 4. Architecture Record

Document service/environment, PostgreSQL version, deployment model, primary/standby/DR locations, backup repository, WAL archive, HA control plane, DNS/routing, KMS/encryption, secrets/IAM, monitoring, and application dependencies. Mark correlated failure domains explicitly.

## 5. Recovery Responsibility Matrix

Define explicit authority for incident declaration, write fencing, recovery-path selection, PITR-target approval, data-loss acceptance, failover/promotion, DR activation, routing, database/application validation, cutover, failback, and cleanup. Technical operators must not invent business data-loss acceptance.

## 6. Recovery Objectives

Record approved RPO/RTO, authorities, and measurement boundaries. Do not equate backup frequency with RPO, restore runtime with RTO, replication lag with business data loss, or retention with recoverability.

## 7. Backup Inventory and Trust

Inventory logical dumps, physical backups, provider snapshots, WAL archive, protected/immutable copies, and cross-region copies. For each, record type, location, retention, verification, last restore test, and failure-domain independence.

Evaluate shared production credentials, deletion permissions, cloud account/project/subscription, region, KMS, storage control plane, automation, and security-compromise domains. A copy in another bucket is not automatically independent.

## 8. Recovery Evidence Hierarchy

```text
BACKUP EXISTS
      !=
BACKUP VALIDATED
      !=
RESTORE TESTED
      !=
APPLICATION RECOVERED
      !=
DR REHEARSED
      !=
RECOVERY READY
```

`pg_verifybackup` evidence does not replace an isolated restore rehearsal, and an isolated restore does not replace application acceptance.

## 9. WAL and PITR Design

Document archive mechanism/destination/monitoring, base-backup relationship, WAL retention, timeline-history handling, supported PITR targets, and last rehearsal.

A valid PITR path requires a usable base backup, every required WAL segment, required timeline history, a reachable recovery target, and a compatible recovery environment. Explicitly document applicable PostgreSQL recovery target semantics: `recovery_target_time`, `recovery_target_lsn`, `recovery_target_xid`, `recovery_target_name`, or `recovery_target = 'immediate'`, plus `recovery_target_inclusive`, `recovery_target_timeline`, and `recovery_target_action`.

Promotion can create a new timeline; WAL existence alone does not prove the required timeline chain is complete. `archive_mode = on` is configuration evidence, not proof of a complete recovery chain.

## 10. PostgreSQL 18 Evidence Semantics

- Received WAL is not necessarily replayed WAL.
- `pg_last_xact_replay_timestamp()` is not a universal real-time lag metric.
- Replication lag is not direct business RPO evidence.
- Synchronous replication configuration does not prove the expected synchronous standby is currently available.
- Replication-slot inactivity does not mean abandonment.
- `max_slot_wal_keep_size = -1` means **UNBOUNDED SLOT WAL RETENTION**.
- WAL/archive statistics must be interpreted relative to their `stats_reset` baseline.
- Missing, stale, or inaccessible evidence is **UNKNOWN**, not zero or PASS.

## 11. HA Design

Document topology, sync/async behavior, failover mechanism, fencing, endpoint/routing behavior, slots, expected lag, failover authority, and last rehearsal. Replication is not an independent historical backup.

## 12. Recovery Artifact Compatibility

Physical backup recovery must respect PostgreSQL physical-format/major-version requirements; it is not a general cross-major upgrade mechanism. Logical recovery has different portability characteristics but still requires compatibility validation. Per-database logical dumps do not automatically provide every cluster-global dependency, such as roles and tablespace definitions.

For PostgreSQL 18 incremental physical backups, record the complete dependency/reconstruction chain rather than treating the newest incremental artifact as necessarily standalone.

## 13. DR Design

Document alternate compute, storage, network, DNS, IAM, KMS, secrets, PostgreSQL binaries, extensions/packages, tablespaces, backup/WAL access, monitoring, application dependencies, and operators. A remote backup without usable recovery infrastructure is not a complete DR plan.

## 14. Capacity Model

Model backup and recovery capacity separately. Include full/base and incremental dependencies, logical backups, WAL, retention overlap, verification artifacts, growth, restored database, replay, workspace, decompression, rebuild overhead, logs, application startup, and operational reserve. Use measured throughput where possible; missing measurements are UNKNOWN.

## 15. Recovery Dependency Package

Maintain protected mappings for non-default tablespaces and extensions. Include storage destination/mount/capacity/permissions/ownership/mapping, and extension catalog version plus compatible package/binary/shared libraries/configuration. Generic SAFE-READ evidence intentionally minimizes sensitive topology.

## 16. Pre-Rehearsal Gate

Verify isolated/disposable target approval, protected recovery source/fallback, compatible PostgreSQL and extensions, tablespace dependencies, KMS/secrets/IAM, storage/network capacity, required WAL/timeline chain, fenced side effects, controlled schedulers/workers, evidence location, cleanup owner, GitOps/operator/controller behavior, and protection from accidental production endpoint exposure.

## 17. Rehearsal Authority and Isolation

SAFE-READ collection, isolated recovery rehearsal, and production recovery are separate authority boundaries. The SQL artifact never authorizes promotion, restore, routing, slot mutation, or cutover.

Fence Kafka/CDC/Debezium, email/SMS, webhooks, payments, APIs, ETL, search indexing, workers, cron/`pg_cron`, Kubernetes CronJobs, and schedulers before restored applications start. Restored production data remains production-sensitive.

## 18. Rehearsal Safety Boundary

The rehearsal must not overwrite production, create dual authoritative writers, destroy the only recovery copy, manually delete `pg_wal`, automatically mutate slots, expose restored sensitive data unnecessarily, or reconnect historical data to live side-effecting systems without approval.

## 19. Split-Brain and Control-Plane Gate

DNS/routing is not fencing. Fence old and alternate write paths before enabling a new authoritative writer. Coordinate PostgreSQL operators, StatefulSets/PVC automation, health probes, Argo CD/Flux, Terraform/configuration automation, and other reconcilers. Any reconciliation suspension must be authorized, recorded, and restored.

## 20. Recovery Target Record

Record target type/value, timezone/offset, inclusive/exclusive choice, timeline, target action, source evidence, operator, independent verifier, approver, and confirmation that the backup/WAL/timeline chain can reach the target. Alert time is not automatically the corruption boundary.

## 21. Restore/Rehearsal Execution Record

For every state-changing action record timestamp, operator, approval, before-state evidence, action/command, target, expected/actual result, after-state evidence, and evidence location. Never store plaintext secrets.

## 22. PostgreSQL and Application Validation

Validate server/version, recovery state, databases, critical schemas/tables, deterministic business checks, roles/grants, extensions, tablespaces, configuration dependencies, expected recent transactions, monitoring/logging, application connectivity/authentication, approved writes, workflows, integrations, queues, workers, jobs, reporting, and monitoring.

`SELECT 1` alone is not recovery validation. A database that starts but fails critical application acceptance is **NOT ACCEPTED** unless authorized ownership explicitly accepts documented degraded operation.

## 23. Measure RPO and RTO

Use business/workload evidence for RPO where possible. LSN distance alone does not prove exact business data loss.

For RTO, record T0 incident/exercise start, T1 declaration, T2 recovery decision, T3 execution start, T4 PostgreSQL available, T5 DB validation complete, T6 application validation complete, and T7 service accepted. Use organizationally approved boundaries.

## 24. HA/Failover and Failback Rehearsal

Where applicable, rehearse failure detection, fencing, candidate readiness, promotion/failover, routing, validation, monitoring, authoritative topology, and fallback. Treat failback as a separate lifecycle: establish authority/divergence/timeline, rebuild/reseed, establish replication, validate synchronization, approve switchover, validate application, monitor, and clean up. Never assume the former primary remains authoritative.

## 25. Evidence Classification and Exception Authority

Recovery evidence is **[SENSITIVE OPERATIONAL EVIDENCE]**. Protect database names, replication/application identifiers, slot names, architecture, backup locations, failure domains, and procedures. Never store passwords, tokens, private keys, cloud access keys, credential-bearing connection strings, or KMS key material.

`N/A` requires an explicit architectural reason; not tested is normally `UNKNOWN`. Material exceptions require risk, owner, remediation, target date, temporary control where applicable, and authorized acceptance.

## 26. Security-Incident Variant

Preserve forensic evidence, determine compromise window, identify trusted recovery artifacts, validate KMS/IAM/secrets trust, rotate compromised credentials as required, recover into trusted isolation, validate data/security state, and reconnect only after authorization. A technically intact backup may still contain compromised state.

## 27. Managed PostgreSQL and Kubernetes Variants

For managed services, document provider backup/PITR behavior, snapshot scope, retention, cross-region recovery, restore behavior, KMS, quotas, control-plane dependency, failover semantics, and extension restrictions.

For Kubernetes, distinguish pod restart, node replacement, PVC recovery, DB failover, backup restore, cluster recreation, and regional DR. Coordinate manual recovery with the operator/controller.

## 28. Evidence Freshness

Record dates and architecture/version for backup validation, restore/PITR tests, failover, DR, failback, and application recovery. Reassess after PostgreSQL upgrades, major data growth, extension/backup/KMS/cloud/HA/DR/operator/application changes.

## 29. Recovery Acceptance Matrix and Exceptions

Track backup validation, WAL/PITR coverage, restore rehearsal, application recovery, RPO, RTO, HA, DR, failback, and dependencies with evidence, result, owner, and exception. Never convert missing evidence to PASS.

Every exception records risk, owner, remediation, target date, temporary control, and acceptance authority.

## 30. Fail-Closed Automation

Stop recovery automation on missing WAL, unexpected timeline, failed verification, ambiguous target, unavailable KMS/secrets, insufficient capacity, missing extension dependency, or failed application validation. Do not silently choose another source or perform destructive cleanup.

## 31. Cleanup Gate

After acceptance, preserve evidence and fallback through the approved window; confirm authoritative topology; sanitize/destroy temporary restored data; remove temporary infrastructure/network changes; revoke emergency access; restore GitOps/operator reconciliation; confirm backup, WAL archive, replication where designed, monitoring, alerts, retention, and protected recovery copies; assign exceptions. Do not destroy evidence required for active forensic/legal work.

## 32. Final Recovery Readiness Statement

Record service, assessment date, architecture/version, approved and observed RPO/RTO, last restore/PITR/application/HA/DR/failback tests, open exceptions/owners, evidence package, decision authority, and next rehearsal.

The SQL artifact must never assign organizational READY/NOT READY status. Decision terminology follows organizational policy and authorized governance.

## 33. Project Completion Criteria

Completion requires documented architecture/failure domains, approved RPO/RTO, inventoried/validated recovery sources, PITR dependencies, HA/DR boundaries, capacity, isolated recovery rehearsal, PostgreSQL/application validation, measured results, exceptions/remediation, evidence preservation, safe cleanup, and next rehearsal scheduling.

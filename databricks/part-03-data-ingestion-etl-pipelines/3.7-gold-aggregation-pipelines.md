# Part 3.7 — Gold Aggregation Pipelines

**Track:** Databricks Production Tutorials  
**Part:** 3 — Data Ingestion & ETL/ELT Pipelines  
**Status:** Revised Final / Canonical Edition

## Learning objectives

By the end of this tutorial, you should be able to:

- explain the operational boundary between Silver and Gold;
- define a metric contract before writing aggregation code;
- define and validate Gold grain;
- distinguish additive measures from non-additive distinct measures;
- define business time and timezone semantics explicitly;
- validate population filters, NULL policies, and join cardinality;
- explain late-data and historical-correction requirements;
- choose deliberately between Gold tables, views, and materialized views;
- distinguish technical freshness from business freshness;
- version metric semantics and preserve historical comparability;
- reconcile Gold results according to metric semantics;
- keep writable transformation validation separate from SAFE-READ acceptance.

## 1. Gold in the medallion architecture

~~~text
SOURCE
   |
   v
BRONZE
   |
   v
SILVER
   |
   v
trusted detailed representation
   |
   v
GOLD
   |
   v
business models / metrics / serving datasets
~~~

The governing boundary is:

~~~text
Bronze = source-oriented evidence
Silver = trusted domain-level data
Gold   = business-oriented serving model
~~~

Gold is not limited to aggregation. It can contain dimensional models, measures, business-facing datasets, and precomputed summaries.

## 2. Begin with the business question

Do not begin a Gold model with an unexplained `COUNT(*)`.

Start with:

~~~text
business question
      |
      v
metric definition
      |
      v
grain
      |
      v
population
      |
      v
dimensions
      |
      v
time + timezone
      |
      v
NULL policy
      |
      v
aggregation semantics
      |
      v
freshness expectation
~~~

Correct SQL does not automatically mean a correct metric.

## 3. Tutorial metric contract

This tutorial uses synthetic patient-activity data.

~~~text
Business question:
How many patient-activity events occurred
per facility, event type, and business date?

Gold object:
training.gold.daily_patient_activity

Grain:
activity_date
+ facility_code
+ event_type

Measures:
event_count
unique_patients

Population:
validated Silver patient-activity records

Time basis:
event_timestamp

Timezone:
explicit approved business timezone

NULL policy:
defined explicitly by the metric contract
~~~

## 4. Grain is a production contract

For this model, one logical Gold row represents:

~~~text
activity_date
+
facility_code
+
event_type
~~~

Therefore an acceptance check should find:

~~~text
duplicate Gold grain = 0
~~~

unless the model explicitly defines another differentiating attribute.

A duplicate grain can indicate replay, write, grouping, or modeling defects.

## 5. Business time is not processing time

A pipeline may expose several clocks:

~~~text
event_timestamp
_ingested_at
_silver_processed_at
_gold_processed_at
~~~

The tutorial metric uses business event time.

~~~text
activity_date
=
business-date(event_timestamp)
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
business event time
       !=
pipeline processing time
~~~

## 6. Timezone is part of the metric definition

A UTC timestamp near midnight can belong to another calendar date in the approved business timezone.

~~~text
event_timestamp
      |
      v
approved business timezone
      |
      v
activity_date
~~~

Do not let an accidental session/default timezone define the business day.

For the writable lab, choose and record an explicit training timezone. Production systems must use the timezone approved by the metric owner.

## 7. Basic aggregation pattern

Conceptually:

~~~python
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

gold_df = (
    silver_df
    .withColumn(
        "activity_date",
        F.to_date("event_timestamp")
    )
    .groupBy(
        "activity_date",
        "facility_code",
        "event_type"
    )
    .agg(
        F.count("*").alias("event_count"),
        F.countDistinct("patient_id").alias("unique_patients")
    )
)
~~~

The example focuses on aggregation mechanics. A production implementation must apply the approved timezone conversion before deriving `activity_date`.

## 8. Event counts and patient counts are different measures

Suppose Silver contains:

~~~text
P001 -> ADMISSION
P001 -> TRANSFER
P002 -> ADMISSION
~~~

Then:

~~~text
event_count     = 3
unique_patients = 2
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
events
   !=
patients
~~~

Metric names and definitions must make this distinction clear.

## 9. Additive versus non-additive measures

An event count is generally additive across compatible, non-overlapping groups.

~~~text
event_count
     |
     v
often additive
~~~

A distinct patient count is generally not safely additive across overlapping groups.

~~~text
unique_patients
     |
     v
non-additive / context-dependent
~~~

Example:

~~~text
ADMISSION
P001
P002

TRANSFER
P001
~~~

Per-group distinct counts are:

~~~text
2 + 1 = 3
~~~

but the distinct population across both groups is:

~~~text
P001
P002

= 2
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
SUM(group-level DISTINCT)
        !=
DISTINCT across groups
~~~

Do not expose a distinct metric without documenting how consumers may aggregate it.

## 10. Gold population must be explicit

Suppose:

~~~text
Silver input    = 1,000,000
Gold population =   850,000
~~~

The missing 150,000 records require an explainable business rule.

~~~text
Silver
   |
   v
documented population filter
   |
   v
Gold metric population
~~~

Do not hide metric semantics inside an unexplained `WHERE` clause.

## 11. NULL dimensions require policy

Suppose:

~~~text
facility_code = NULL
~~~

Possible policies include:

~~~text
UNKNOWN
UNASSIGNED
exclude under an approved rule
upstream remediation
~~~

No policy is universally correct.

The rule is:

~~~text
NULL handling
     =
metric-contract decision
~~~

For the synthetic lab, unexpected NULL values in the Gold grain are not allowed.

## 12. Gold can join trusted Silver datasets

A business model may combine:

~~~text
Silver activity
       +
Silver facility dimension
       |
       v
Gold analytical model
~~~

For example, a facility dimension can add:

~~~text
facility_name
region
market
~~~

But every join can change metric cardinality.

## 13. Join amplification risk

Suppose:

~~~text
fact rows before join = 1,000
~~~

and a supposedly many-to-one dimension join produces:

~~~text
fact rows after join = 1,025
~~~

That is a warning.

Validate:

~~~text
fact count before join
fact count after join
dimension-key uniqueness
duplicate dimension keys
unmatched fact keys
expected join cardinality
~~~

A duplicate dimension key can silently inflate downstream measures.

~~~text
dimension integrity
       |
       v
join integrity
       |
       v
metric integrity
~~~

## 14. Reconciliation follows metric semantics

For this tutorial, every Silver event contributes exactly once to `event_count`.

Therefore:

~~~text
Silver event count
       =
SUM(Gold.event_count)
~~~

is a useful lab reconciliation.

But:

~~~text
global unique patients
       =
SUM(Gold.unique_patients)
~~~

is not generally valid.

The canonical principle is:

~~~text
reconciliation equation
       derives from
metric semantics
~~~

## 15. Late-arriving Silver data changes history

Suppose September 18 Gold was calculated and a legitimate September 18 Silver event arrives on September 19.

~~~text
historical Gold grain
       |
       v
potentially stale
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
Gold completed
     !=
Gold immutable
~~~

A production Gold pipeline needs an explicit late-data correction strategy.

## 16. Historical correction strategies

Two broad patterns are:

~~~text
late/corrected Silver
        |
        v
identify affected Gold grain/period
        |
        v
recompute bounded result
~~~

or a correctly designed incremental update.

Part 3.7 establishes the boundary. Detailed MERGE-based incremental implementation is covered later in Part 3.11.

For many aggregates, recomputing a bounded affected period from trusted Silver can be easier to reason about than inverse adjustments.

## 17. Replay must not double count

Avoid:

~~~text
replay Silver
     |
     v
append Gold aggregate again
     |
     v
double count
~~~

Instead:

~~~text
Silver population
      +
existing Gold state
      +
target grain
      +
write strategy
      |
      v
controlled reconstruction
~~~

Replay and backfill behavior must be part of the operational design.

## 18. Gold serving choices

Gold can be served through different governed objects depending on requirements.

~~~text
Gold requirement
      |
      +-------------------+
      |                   |
      v                   v
Delta table        Materialized view
      |
      +--> logical view can also fit
           non-persisted serving needs
~~~

Choose according to freshness, performance, transformation complexity, refresh behavior, compute capabilities, and operational ownership.

## 19. Materialized views

Materialized views are useful for precomputed Gold summaries and analytical serving.

~~~text
Silver
   |
   v
business aggregation
   |
   v
Materialized View
   |
   v
BI / reporting / applications
~~~

But:

~~~text
Materialized View
       !=
mandatory Gold implementation
~~~

## 20. Incremental-refresh boundary

Do not assume:

~~~text
Materialized View
      =
always incremental
~~~

Instead:

~~~text
materialized view
       |
       v
refresh planning
       |
       v
incremental refresh
when eligible and supported
~~~

Current platform capabilities and compute requirements must be checked for the target environment. Refresh behavior can change when query shape or platform conditions change.

## 21. Refresh success does not prove metric correctness

A successful refresh proves neither business semantics nor reconciliation.

~~~text
refresh SUCCESS
      !=
grain correct
      !=
population correct
      !=
join correct
      !=
distinct semantics correct
      !=
business data current
~~~

Gold needs both pipeline and metric validation.

## 22. Technical freshness versus business freshness

~~~text
TECHNICAL FRESHNESS
=
when the Gold object last refreshed successfully
~~~

versus:

~~~text
BUSINESS FRESHNESS
=
latest expected business data represented
~~~

A table refreshed at 10:00 can still contain business data only through 07:00.

Therefore:

~~~text
recent refresh
      !=
current business data
~~~

## 23. Metric-definition changes are data changes

Suppose:

~~~text
unique_patients v1
       |
       v
definition A
~~~

changes to:

~~~text
unique_patients v2
       |
       v
definition B
~~~

This is not merely a code refactor.

Decide whether to:

~~~text
rebuild history
version the Gold model
preserve the old metric
apply prospectively
~~~

Record the effective date and historical-treatment decision.

## 24. Protect historical comparability

Do not silently combine:

~~~text
Jan-Aug -> definition A
Sep-Dec -> definition B
~~~

into a continuous trend as though the metric meaning never changed.

Metric changes require validation and consumer communication.

## 25. Transformation traceability

Production responders should be able to identify:

~~~text
Silver source identity/version
Gold transformation version
metric-definition version
refresh/execution identity
Gold target/version
~~~

The implementation is environment-specific; the operational requirement is reproducibility and explainability.

## 26. Gold governance

Gold is business-facing, but:

~~~text
Gold
   !=
unrestricted access
~~~

Apply Unity Catalog governance appropriate to the resulting data. Production designs can use approved object privileges and centralized row/column controls where required.

The training lab does not create production security policies.

## 27. Aggregated does not mean anonymous

~~~text
aggregated
   !=
automatically non-sensitive
~~~

Small groups or combinations such as facility, rare condition, and narrow time window can remain sensitive even when direct identifiers are absent.

Apply least privilege according to the resulting dataset.

## 28. Gold observability

Monitor two independent planes.

~~~text
PIPELINE HEALTH
---------------
run/refresh status
duration
failures
compute behavior
upstream availability
~~~

and:

~~~text
METRIC HEALTH
-------------
grain uniqueness
population
NULL dimensions
join amplification
metric ranges
unexpected zeroes
volume changes
reconciliation
business freshness
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
pipeline healthy
      !=
metric healthy
~~~

## 31. Cleanup

Cleanup must target only exact disposable resources created by the lab.

Do not broadly delete or modify:

- production/shared Silver data;
- production/shared Gold data;
- catalogs or shared schemas;
- Volumes or external locations;
- unrelated materialized views;
- pipeline/checkpoint state;
- security configuration.

Cleanup is intentionally outside the canonical SAFE-READ artifact.

## 32. SAFE-READ acceptance

The companion artifact is:

~~~text
labs/gold-aggregation-pipeline-check.sql
~~~

and is classified:

~~~text
[TUTORIAL-ACCEPTANCE — SAFE-READ]
~~~

It performs namespace and metadata inspection only.

It intentionally does not:

- execute Gold aggregation;
- create or modify Silver/Gold objects;
- refresh a materialized view;
- insert, update, delete, or merge data;
- start a pipeline or streaming query;
- manipulate checkpoint/state;
- change privileges;
- write/delete cloud storage;
- perform cleanup.

Remember:

~~~text
SAFE-READ PASS
      !=
metric correctness proven
      !=
Gold grain uniqueness proven
      !=
join cardinality proven
      !=
aggregation reconciliation proven
      !=
distinct-count semantics proven
      !=
late-data handling proven
      !=
business freshness proven
      !=
metric definition approved
~~~

## 33. Key takeaways

1. Gold is a business-oriented serving layer, not merely an aggregation layer.
2. Define the metric contract before implementing the query.
3. Gold grain is a production correctness contract.
4. Business event time and processing time are different.
5. Timezone policy is part of daily metric semantics.
6. Additive and distinct measures behave differently.
7. Population filters and NULL policies must be explicit.
8. Validate join cardinality before trusting measures.
9. Reconciliation rules derive from metric semantics.
10. Late Silver data can make historical Gold stale.
11. Replay must not append aggregate results blindly.
12. Materialized views are useful but not mandatory.
13. Do not assume every materialized-view refresh is incremental.
14. Technical freshness and business freshness are different.
15. Metric-definition changes require versioning/change control.
16. Aggregation does not automatically remove sensitivity.
17. Monitor metric health independently from pipeline health.
18. SAFE-READ metadata validation does not prove business correctness.

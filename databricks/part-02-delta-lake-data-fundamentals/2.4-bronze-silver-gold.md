# Part 2.4 — Bronze, Silver & Gold

**Track:** Databricks Production Tutorials
**Part:** 2 — Delta Lake & Data Fundamentals
**Status:** Revised Final / Canonical Edition

## Learning objectives

By the end of this tutorial, you should be able to:

- explain the Bronze, Silver, and Gold layers of the medallion architecture;
- distinguish raw ingestion from validated data and business-ready data;
- understand why the layers represent logical quality boundaries rather than mandatory schema names;
- design replayable and idempotent processing;
- handle duplicates, malformed records, and late-arriving data deliberately;
- reconcile row counts without assuming every layer must contain the same number of rows;
- understand operational considerations for backfills, data quality, lineage, and sensitive data;
- troubleshoot where data diverged in a multi-layer pipeline.

## 1. Medallion architecture

Medallion architecture organizes data into progressively refined layers.

```text
Source Systems
     |
     v
Bronze
Raw / minimally processed
     |
     | validation + cleansing
     v
Silver
Validated / standardized / integrated
     |
     | business rules + aggregation
     v
Gold
Business-ready consumption
```

Bronze, Silver, and Gold are architectural concepts. They do not require every organization to create schemas literally named `bronze`, `silver`, and `gold`.

The physical catalog and schema design should follow the organization's governance, ownership, environment, and access model.

## 2. Bronze — preserve source fidelity

Bronze is the ingestion boundary.

Its main goals are to preserve source fidelity, ingestion history, and enough metadata to support replay, audit, troubleshooting, and downstream rebuilding.

A healthcare-style example might be:

```text
source patient activity
        |
        v
bronze.patient_activity_raw
```

Useful ingestion metadata can include:

- source identifier;
- source event identifier;
- ingestion timestamp;
- source filename or stream metadata where appropriate;
- arrival timestamp;
- batch/run identifier;
- raw payload or source-aligned columns.

Avoid aggressive business cleansing in Bronze. If raw records are modified destructively, recovery and replay become harder.

## 3. Silver — establish trusted detail

Silver is the primary quality and integration boundary.

Typical Silver processing includes:

- data-type conversion;
- schema validation;
- null handling;
- normalization;
- deterministic deduplication;
- malformed-record handling;
- standardization;
- joins and enrichment;
- late/out-of-order event handling;
- data-quality rules.

Example:

```text
bronze.patient_activity_raw
        |
        +-- validate patient_id
        +-- cast event_timestamp
        +-- standardize event_type
        +-- classify malformed records
        +-- deduplicate deterministically
        |
        v
silver.patient_activity
```

Silver should make its quality rules observable. Invalid records should not silently disappear.

## 4. Gold — business-ready data

Gold represents data shaped for a specific consumption purpose.

Typical Gold use cases include:

- reporting;
- dashboards;
- business metrics;
- dimensional models;
- aggregates;
- application-serving datasets;
- analytics and ML features where appropriate.

Example:

```text
silver.patient_activity
        |
        +-- group by activity_date
        +-- count activity
        +-- count unique patients
        +-- apply reporting definitions
        |
        v
gold.patient_activity_daily
```

Gold is not merely "cleaner Silver." It expresses business semantics.

## 5. Logical layers, not mandatory names

A valid implementation might use:

```text
clinical.bronze.patient_activity_raw
clinical.silver.patient_activity
clinical.gold.patient_activity_daily
```

Another organization may use different catalogs and schemas while preserving the same logical progression.

Do not make production automation depend on the words Bronze, Silver, or Gold unless those names are part of the organization's formal namespace contract.

## 6. Batch and streaming

Medallion architecture is not limited to batch ETL.

```text
Batch ------+
            +----> Bronze -> Silver -> Gold
Streaming --+
```

The processing technology can differ while the quality boundaries remain useful.

Streaming pipelines additionally need careful handling of checkpoints, watermarks, late events, retries, and state.

## 7. Managed vs external is a separate decision

Do not infer storage ownership from the Medallion layer.

For example, "Bronze" does not automatically mean external, and "Gold" does not automatically mean managed.

Part 2.3 covers managed and external table lifecycle. Medallion architecture describes the role and quality of data, while managed/external describes storage and lifecycle ownership.

## 8. Hands-on scenario

> **LAB SAFETY:** The following exercise changes database state. Use only disposable synthetic data in an approved training environment.

Assume the following logical objects:

```text
bronze.patient_activity_raw
        |
        v
silver.patient_activity
        |
        v
gold.patient_activity_daily
```

Use synthetic records only. Never place real patient information in tutorial datasets.

### Bronze example

A raw record might preserve source-aligned values:

```text
event_id        = EVT-1001
patient_id      = P-100
event_type      = " VISIT "
event_timestamp = "2026-09-18T14:10:00Z"
ingested_at     = ingestion timestamp
```

Bronze should preserve enough information to explain what arrived.

### Silver transformation

Silver can:

- trim and standardize `event_type`;
- parse `event_timestamp`;
- validate required identifiers;
- apply deterministic duplicate handling;
- separate invalid records from accepted records.

Example result:

```text
event_id        = EVT-1001
patient_id      = P-100
event_type      = VISIT
event_timestamp = valid timestamp
```

### Gold transformation

Gold might produce daily aggregates:

```text
activity_date
activity_count
unique_patients
```

The metric definitions must be documented so consumers understand exactly what is being counted.

## 9. Deterministic deduplication

Do not teach `SELECT DISTINCT` as a universal production deduplication strategy.

A robust rule should answer:

```text
What identifies the same event?
Which record wins?
What timestamp/version determines the winner?
What happens when the winner changes?
```

For example, a pipeline might use a stable source event ID and retain the latest version according to an authoritative source sequence or timestamp.

The exact rule is domain-specific and must be documented.

## 10. Bad-record handling

Invalid records should produce evidence.

A useful conceptual flow is:

```text
Bronze
   |
   +---- valid ----------------> Silver
   |
   +---- invalid -------------> quarantine / quality evidence
```

A quarantine mechanism should capture enough information to investigate and potentially replay the record while respecting security requirements.

Do not use a quarantine table as an uncontrolled permanent dump of sensitive raw data.

## 11. Late-arriving data

Events do not always arrive in business-time order.

A record for yesterday can arrive today. This means a previously calculated Gold aggregate might need correction.

Production design should define:

- acceptable lateness;
- event time versus ingestion time;
- watermark behavior where streaming is used;
- how Silver incorporates late records;
- whether Gold is incrementally corrected or recomputed;
- when historical periods become operationally closed.

## 12. Idempotency and replay

A production rerun should not silently multiply data.

A replay-safe design normally needs stable identifiers and deterministic transformation rules.

Before a rerun or backfill, answer:

```text
What data range will be read?
What data range will be written?
Can the operation be repeated safely?
How are duplicates prevented?
How is partial failure recovered?
How is success validated?
```

Blind repeated `INSERT` operations are not a safe general backfill strategy.

## 13. Row-count reconciliation

Do not require:

```text
Bronze count = Silver count = Gold count
```

as a universal acceptance rule.

Consider:

```text
Source records       1,000,000
Bronze               1,000,000
Silver                 997,500
Gold                    24,000
```

This can be valid.

Silver may have classified duplicate or invalid records. Gold may aggregate many detailed rows into one business-level row.

A better reconciliation model is:

```text
Bronze input
    =
Silver accepted
  + rejected/quarantined
  + intentional duplicate outcomes
  + other documented quality outcomes
```

Then validate Gold according to its transformation and aggregation contract.

## 14. Backfill safety

Before a production backfill:

```text
[ ] Confirm source range.
[ ] Confirm Bronze availability.
[ ] Identify transformation version.
[ ] Confirm deduplication key/rule.
[ ] Confirm late-data behavior.
[ ] Identify Gold dependencies.
[ ] Define expected write scope.
[ ] Verify idempotency.
[ ] Prepare validation queries.
[ ] Assess consumer impact.
[ ] Define recovery/rollback.
```

Backfills should be controlled production changes.

## 15. Sensitive data and healthcare examples

Bronze can contain the highest-fidelity representation of source data and can therefore contain sensitive information.

Apply least privilege, appropriate retention, auditing, encryption, and organizational data-handling requirements.

Gold does not automatically mean public or non-sensitive. A refined dataset can still contain protected information.

All examples in this tutorial are synthetic.

## 16. Gold metric governance

A Gold column such as:

```text
daily_active_patients
```

needs a business definition.

If that definition changes, determine:

```text
definition change
       |
       v
affected history
       |
       v
backfill/recompute?
       |
       v
consumer validation
```

Otherwise the same metric name can represent different logic across time.

## 17. Layer ownership and consumers

A common pattern is:

```text
BI / reporting ---------> Gold
Detailed analytics -----> Silver
Recovery / replay ------> Bronze
```

This is guidance, not a rigid restriction. A consumer can legitimately use another layer when the contract requires it.

The important point is that dependencies should be intentional and documented.

## 18. DBRE/SRE troubleshooting

If a Gold dashboard suddenly reports fewer records, start from evidence rather than immediately rerunning the entire pipeline.

Investigate:

1. Did the expected source records arrive?
2. Did Bronze ingest them?
3. Did schema or source format change?
4. How many Silver records were accepted?
5. How many were rejected or quarantined?
6. How many duplicates were intentionally removed?
7. Were late records involved?
8. Did business rules change?
9. Did Gold aggregation logic change?
10. Was a backfill or partial rerun performed?
11. Are consumers querying the expected environment and table version?

This narrows the failing boundary.

## 19. Production operational checklist

```text
[ ] Confirm environment and namespace.
[ ] Confirm source completeness.
[ ] Confirm Bronze ingestion evidence.
[ ] Confirm Silver quality outcomes.
[ ] Reconcile rejected/quarantined records.
[ ] Validate deterministic deduplication.
[ ] Review late-arriving records.
[ ] Confirm transformation version.
[ ] Validate Gold business definitions.
[ ] Check backfill/rerun history.
[ ] Confirm lineage and downstream consumers.
[ ] Protect sensitive data at every layer.
```


Object-specific count and reconciliation queries should be run separately only after the operator has identified approved targets.

## 21. Key takeaways

1. Medallion architecture organizes data into progressively trusted logical layers.
2. Bronze prioritizes source fidelity, history, and replayability.
3. Silver is the main validation, cleansing, standardization, and integration boundary.
4. Gold represents business-oriented consumption and semantics.
5. Bronze/Silver/Gold are not mandatory physical schema names.
6. Medallion architecture works with batch and streaming pipelines.
7. Managed-versus-external storage is a separate design decision.
8. Deduplication must be deterministic.
9. Invalid records should produce observable quality outcomes rather than silently disappear.
10. Late data can require correction of downstream results.
11. Row counts do not need to remain equal across layers.
12. Reruns and backfills should be idempotent and validated.
13. Sensitive information must remain protected at every layer.
14. Gold metrics require documented business definitions.
15. SAFE-READ acceptance remains separate from transformation and cleanup.
